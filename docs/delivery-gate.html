01 Why this gate exists

What the sample project actually proves

Read past the PySpark and the sample makes an argument about where the marks live. It is worth stating plainly, because it is not what an engineer would assume.

The sample — Predictive Workforce Analytics, on Singapore's Graduate Employment Survey — spends thirteen notebook Parts marching through HDFS, MapReduce, Pig, Hive, Sqoop, Kafka, HBase and Spark before it trains a single model. Then it trains eight. Then it throws the best classifier away in public: 81.6% accuracy against an 80.9% no-skill baseline, recall 98.7%, specificity 9.0%. The deck calls this "an 81.6% accurate model that is nearly useless" and gives it a full slide.

And it still closes on five decisions, each carrying the number that forces it: retire the single salary band (S$3,000 between-faculty spread), quote medians not means (S$329 gap, 57.4% of the cohort below), add three faculties to the recruiting circuit (38–46% unplaced), drop the GPA cut-off (21.0% of signal versus 70.3%), publish intervals (twelve programmes under thirty respondents).

The reframe The models are scaffolding. The terminal artifact is a decision surface a manager can act on. Your project is currently strong exactly where the sample is weak — and untested exactly where the sample is strong.
Verified — read from the files, 12 Aug 2026 sample project/
  • The aggregate statistics are genuinely real. Six programmes spot-checked against Web-Publication-NUS-GES-2024.pdf — Arts, Law, Computer Science, Biomedical Eng, Music, BBA — all match the MOE publication exactly.
  • The individuals are not. 4,340 profiles are sampled from log-normals fitted to each programme's published quartiles. The per-programme cohort sizes that decide how many synthetic people each degree gets are invented — they appear nowhere in the MOE PDF.
  • The notebook ships with zero saved outputs. All 41 code cells unrun. Every figure in the deck — 70.3%, R² 0.543, AUC 0.731 — traces to a run that is not preserved anywhere in the folder. This is the one thing you must not copy.
  • The .py is the notebook. Byte-for-byte the same code cells concatenated — diffed and confirmed. Not a second artifact.
  • The headline finding is partly designed in. The generator's own comment reads "degree sets the salary BAND; GPA and internships shift position WITHIN it." So "degree explains 70.3%, GPA 21%" substantially restates the simulation's parameters. The deck presents it as a discovery.
Where you already win The sample runs on 4,340 synthetic rows. Yours runs on 21,556,435 real hands and 116.6M seat-rows, reconciling to the published count exactly. Its data is manufactured because the real thing was unavailable; yours is the real thing. Do not let that advantage go unstated — and do not squander it by shipping an unrun notebook, which is the one place the sample would beat you.
02 Cross-cutting

The five laws

These bind every artifact. If a chart, slide, paragraph or dashboard panel breaks one of them, it is wrong regardless of how good the underlying engineering is.

I
Every finding ends in a decision, and every decision carries its number.
Not "grinders multi-table more." Instead: "Cap simultaneous tables at six — the top 2% of players by table count extract X bb/100 from recreational seats, worth ₹Y of monthly rake." Who acts, what changes, and the figure that forces it.
Sample: five recommendations, every one numbered. "Retire the single graduate band — between-faculty spread exceeds S$3,000."
II
No metric appears without its no-skill baseline beside it.
An accuracy score alone is not evidence. Churn classifier vs. "predict everyone lapses"; regression vs. predicting the mean; K-Means silhouette vs. random assignment. If the model does not beat the baseline, that is a finding — report it as one.
Sample: 81.6% against an 80.9% baseline — the entire slide exists to make that comparison impossible to miss.
III
Every number on every artifact traces to a cell that ran and saved its output.
If a figure is on a slide, you can point at the notebook cell that produced it, with the output still attached. No hand-typed numbers, no "I ran it once." The examiner will pick one number and ask where it came from.
Sample: fails this outright — 41 unrun cells. Learn it as a counterexample.
IV
Segment the population before you model it — and prove it is not one population.
Show the two distributions that barely overlap. For you that is recreational vs. grinder, and it is also six venues that do not share an observation window or money coverage. A single global model over all six is the poker equivalent of one company-wide salary band.
Sample: "The graduate market is not one market. It is two." Computing sits almost entirely above S$5,500; Arts between S$3,800 and S$5,200.
V
Lead with the failure, and name the variable you wish you had.
Find the thing that did not work, put it on its own slide, and explain why the failure is more useful than a flattering number. Then name the missing data: 77.6% of hole cards are never revealed; iPoker records no table identity on any of its 6.0M hands; there is no ground truth for bots.
Sample: "This is the result we almost reported as a success." Then names internship quality as the missing variable.

12 Ship

The 72-hour pre-flight

Mechanical, unglamorous, and the source of most avoidable losses. Do these in the last three days, in this order.

  1. Restart-and-run-all the notebook, watch it finish, and confirm the outputs are saved in the committed file.
  2. Count the slides. Including title and thank-you. Ten or fewer.
  3. Open the dashboard on a machine that is not yours, from the file, with no server running.
  4. Export the report to PDF and read it in the exported form — tables break, figures move, headings orphan.
  5. Grep every deliverable for the word "churn" and replace with short-horizon lapse.
  6. Pick three numbers at random from the deck and trace each to its notebook cell. If any one fails, assume others will too.
  7. Read the appendix checklist: references, AI-usage disclosure, team contributions. All three.
  8. Push, then clone the repo fresh into a new folder and follow your own README from scratch.
